Eorl Auwsy SY-Z d13-450, 3312 AD

Jasmina Halsey stood in front of the crystal window in the Odysseus’ starboard observation lounge, immersed and nearly motionless, consuming the blackness beyond with her eyes.

And possibly with her soul, Hermine Delarue thought.

She had entered the lounge silently and noticed the subtle trembling in Jasmina’s left hand. It had started shortly after they left Rainbow’s End a few weeks ago. And ever since she had found her in the observation lounge, standing there for hours, trying to sense the cosmos.

“Anything?” Hermine asked.

Jasmina’s answer came an eternity later: “No. Nothing.”

Hermine moved a few steps closer but said nothing.

“Maybe we should have stayed a few days longer. We should have made sure everything was in order. I mean we didn’t even bother to double check on the systems we installed at Rainbow’s End. The Galcop array, the shielding…”

She slowly turned around, a mixture between doubt and despair written all over her face.

“What if it didn’t last?”

Hermine considered that for a moment.

“You insisted we leave immediately after we filled Rainbow’s memory buffers with all that exploration data. We made sure our lattice signature was buried under petabytes of stellar surveys. Uninitiated station crew will not be able to find it. It’s as secure as we could make it given the circumstances.”

“I know,” Jasmina replied. “Still, I would have wished for a confirmation from our Guardian friend that everything was in order. A thank you, if you want to put it that way.”

“Jas, did you really think a million year old non-human AI who probably has seen a few stars explode and lives in hyperspace would say so long and thanks for all the fish?”

“Yes,” she shrugged. “Except for the fish maybe.”

Then, smiling a wry smile, she added: “Call me too human but I think we here, all of us, deserve a thanks. And I thought… I thought when not even our own species has the guts to thank us for all the pain, the hardship and all this space madness, would it be too much for the thing we actually rescued to say okay, nicely done?”

Hermine stared at her for a long moment.

“Well, would it?”

“That’s your job, Jas.”

“What?!”

Hermine remained calm, crossing her arms. “It’s your job. Simple as that. You are the Federal Envoy tasked to oversee an investigation, remember? You may be the AI’s pen friend but your real duty is being a Federal Envoy to the expedition. And to me as a rep of DSS.”

“Are you serious?” 

“I sure am. Staring into the void solves nothing. Neither does hoping you won a friend for life with this one. We need to figure out what all this really means. The wrecks, the matter manipulation, the AI in hyperspace, radio messages hundreds of years old, the myths about the Archer’s Shadow, implications for exploration… What does all this mean for us mortals reaching for the stars?”

“Poetic… You mean what can we actually tell them?”

“Well, for starters, you need to say thanks to all the explorers out there. You of all people. At times we have been honest with them and at times we have been gaslighting them. There is no way to frame it differently and were Lya here she would start babbling about her Snark hunting poem again, I guess.”

Jasmina couldn’t do much more than look at Hermine, doubt slowly making way to more important things like duty and responsibility. They slowly flooded back into her beleaguered mind. 

“Reality catches up with us, Jas. You need to make room for your species again. And you have the obligation to address them and their concerns,” she added. “You cannot assume that AI acts on human scales when it comes to morality and politeness. But all those explorers do.”

“I haven’t heard a voice in my head for weeks now!” Jasmina insisted, her hand still trembling. She made a fist.

“Well, what did you expect? We buried it. And besides, most humans would consider not hearing voices in their heads a good thing.”

“Very well…”

Hermine took another step towards her, resting her hands gently on her shoulders.

“I am here for you, whenever you want to talk. But soon we’ll be nearing the Bubble again and quite frankly, we need your savvy and political acumen to sail those waters. Yes, we have been cleared but only through out-scheming the schemers.”

“Did you erase our records then?” Jasmina suddenly asked. “Flight logs, evac plans, neutron scans, all that goofy stuff? We cannot risk another leak making 8,000 explorers outcasts again. We used up nearly all our clout dealing with the last one and yes, I’m not even sure we’re welcome back home.”

“We aren’t,” Hermine conceded. “Not us decision makers anyway. They’ll give us a standing ovation and some photo ops when we return, praise all our achievements ‘for all mankind’ and how we made history again. But in their minds they will never forget how we blackmailed ourselves back into their good graces.” 

She sighed.

“Have you heard anything from our two elderly gentlemen?” Jasmina asked.

“Nope. Ermitage left a voice message saying he still had to deal with a few contacts of his and from Cal there’s been radio silence since we last spoke and he essentially told me we had no business being where we were.” 

“What do you make of this?”

Hermine weighed her head for a good few moments.

“I think it’s possible that covering up whatever Ram Tah has been up to with his AI experiments may be more difficult than they anticipated. And to be honest, Ermitage was never really involved in the day-to-day operations of this expedition. He entrusted DSS with that and although he is kind of an enigmatic person I think he has been as honest as he could be with us.”

“And Cal?”

She shrugged. “Cal is Cal. There is just no knowing what he pulled or what he was able to pull behind the scenes. During the brief talks and sparring matches we had he always kept his guards up while trying to bring mine down, which I admire in a way.”

“But…?”

“But I can’t shake the feeling we haven’t heard the last from him. He has an agenda and I think he used us. For what I don’t know but I can guess it won’t be a small favour he will inevitably ask.”

“I think both have an agenda, and quite possibly they don’t always align.”

“Well, on that we can agree,” Hermine smiled.

“Any last suggestions then before we leave this place and I begin playing politician again and go to the Odysseus’ hairdresser?”

“Yes, we should take a final look at those Guardian ruins in the Teal Nebula,” Hermine proposed. “Apparently Lya and Nadya had a chat about more data for modelling and those ruins down there are far less explored than the ones near the Bubble.”

“Lya and Nadya?”

“Yes. Although I think it’s just Lya wanting to impress Ram Tah with her stuff. I think she has a crush on him.”

“Well,” Jasmina shrugged. “Not all was bad then during our trip.”

“No, not all,” Hermine smiled broadly. “Shall I get your boots on the ground then?”

“Yes, tell Haddard to ready a ship and an SRV for me.”

“Not necessary. Nadya will take you for a ride. She said she has an SRV bay on her Asp so there you go.”

“Holdstock?” Jasmina winced. “That’s just brilliant, thank you so much…”

The Scarab made its way past the rows of ancient Guardian obelisks, head lights piercing the darkness. Jasmina had never been to a Guardian site before. Sure, all those reports she had consumed when the ruins had first been discovered but nothing compared to driving through a field of accurately placed silent watchers, tombstones or memorials that may or may not hold the secrets to the Guardians’ ultimate fate.

‘You were the Architects of Creation,’ Jasmina thought. ‘Why did you destroy yourselves?’

There were other tracks in the ruins of course. Many pilots had been eager to get their boots and tyres on the ground, because space madness and all. And Lya was right: These Guardian ruins were far less explored than the scattered locations in the Bubble. 

But right now she was alone, at least on the ground. She had ordered Hermine to assign her a time window without any other pilots around. They were all off to one of Alec’s races or something. Holdstock was still there, however. She had asked — no, insisted — to monitor the ride from low orbit in her Asp. At least they agreed the ruins were the last waypoint where they could reasonably find out if the euphemistically named ‘AI situation’ was contained. If the voices were really silent. 

If she was… alone again?

Her left hand began to tremble, the hand that had so often held the Quirium Control Unit.

‘So many things you could have taught us… So many lessons you learned… Mistakes, insights, your wisdom, your visions, your… love and care for the cosmos…’

Jasmina’s last ride in an SRV had been ages ago. A few visits on sponsored races, ground force training camps and in select R&D facilities as Federation President. And she also remembered one or two test drives in what could have become a prestigious ground based CQC but which was ultimately shelved because the cash was needed for augmenting the navy. 

One of the few things she had agreed on with Hudson back in the day.  

“Halsey,” Nadya called through the SRV intercom, interrupting her past memories. “Did you notice the obelisks?”

Perplexed by the sudden intrusion she looked around, seeing nothing but black silhouettes against the starlight. A city of standing stones. 

“No? What of it?”

“It seems the obelisks react to your presence. There seems to be a pattern.”

“How so?”

“You need to see for yourself,” Nadya answered. “Turn around and drive your Scarab back to the last perimeter point through that row of obelisks. I will take visual footage and relay it to you through your vehicle’s neurolink.”

“Why?”

“Not why. Just do it.”

“Okay?”

Startled by Nadya’s insistence, Jasmina turned the Scarab around. There was no point in debating with this woman. Why was she one of the few people who could get under her skin? She barely even knew her.

She drove back the way she came with the rows of obelisks left and right. When she reached the perimeter point where she started she activated her neurolink and went into remote observation mode.

“Now what?”

“I will send you the footage I made. And then delete it. So see for yourself.”

And what Jasmina Halsey saw was jaw dropping: 

From the bird's view she saw her SRV turning around and driving towards the perimeter point. As she saw her SRV passing the rows of obelisks, they reacted on either side, one by one, flashing in their signature turquoise colour for several seconds as mysterious symbols cascaded from top to bottom before going dark again.

“Halsey…”

An honour guard? An acknowledgement?

“Yes. I see it.”

She closed her eyes but the vision didn’t vanish. The neurolink felt warm and in her mind Jasmina Halsey saw it all in the blink of an eye: The obelisks in rhythm, not just here, but across the Milky Way, humming to a singular tune. The ruins were civilisation, on the surface of living orbs, the dust were people, the rocks were towers, reaching into the heavens and into the Connect… it was everywhere. 

And after a small eternity she finally sensed the entity in the Connect that had spoken to her all this time.

You know now. You have purpose. Now do.

In another universe, the universe of corporations, bureaucracy, dealmaking and backstabbing, Hermine Delarue found herself in a telepresence meeting with none other than Cynthia Sideris, Chairwoman of Universal Cartographics. She had even requested a visual broadcast. And said she would pay for it.

“Delly! How good it is to see you.”

The telepresence projector made Sideris sit on a couch, legs crossed, formal dress and holding a porcelain cup. She even smiled somewhat. She had perfected staged appearances long ago.

“Cynthia,” Hermine observed coolly. “Long time no see.”

 “Oh indeed. We must amend that, dear. I hear you will direct your entourage towards civilised space again in the near future. Do you already have any plans for June?”

“I guess I’ll just find my office desks full of paperwork and lecture requests.”

“Splendid! If you want an escape plan, I would invite you to Eridani, maybe have a chat about your findings out there in the wild. ExoCart tells me you even found a big derelict carrier from the DSSA guys and brought it to life again. Wow! You do have some ingenious people out there. I bet their inboxes will be full of job offers once they’re back home.”

“Better job than jail, no?”

“Delly,” Sideris protested, feigning indignance. “I hope you’re not suggesting Universal Cartographics had their hands in this unfortunate misunderstanding? We don’t deal in political amateurism. In fact we made it clear we were fully supportive of your little adventure and would welcome any major uploads to our overflowing databases.”

“I don’t care, Cynthia. And stop this Delly nonsense. What are you, twelve? Yes, we found a DSSA carrier inconveniently stranded uncomfortably close to one of your permit locked sectors.”

Hermine added a shrug: “But you know that, don’t you? You just don’t know yet if we were able to find your Arthurian legend. So stop the charade and let’s get to business, shall we?”

Without so much as a blink or wasting another second the cheerleader was the chairwoman again.

“Very good. So what did you find out?”

“You mean apart from the fact the ship is Eridanus class, built for extended surveys and lab ops and relying on a cryo crew rotation?”

“Yes, exactly.”

“Nothing. Apparently the active crew threw some shanties at Jaques Station, uploaded some of their survey data, probably as payment for their cocktails, and then went off into the unknown.” 

“Hermine,” Sideris shook her head with fake disappointment. “We both know this is not the full story. Bar stories never start or end this way.”

“Enlighten me then. You seem to be better acquainted with bars than I am.”

“Before you so valiantly rescued the DSSA carrier you directed your followers to Rendezvous Point. And interestingly, you seem to have surveyed quite a few K-type stars…”

“And?”

“And? Nothing. It’s just that I think you’re not completely honest with me… with the assignment you were given, specifically asking you to look for the Guinevere.”

“Why K-type stars?” Hermine asked.

“Why not? They are the most promising stars for evolved planetary systems with the biggest habitable zones.”

“So they were looking for habitable worlds?”

“Yes, dear. It’s what survey missions do. Your Distant Things is a prime example. Don’t you even have an Earth 2.0 project or something? Ridiculous.”

“Then why have a cryogenic crew when there really is no need for them in the 3300s anymore? Why have sleepers onboard? It vaguely sounds familiar, I just can’t place it…”

“Internal procedures, dear. I couldn’t possibly comment on that, because it could give our competitors valuable project insights.”

“UniCart doesn’t have competitors.”

Sideris just smiled, her face having turned into a fox having found the henhouse.

“I don’t think it was just goldilocks zones you were after, Cynthia. You could easily buy that data. So it must be something you couldn’t publicly acknowledge you were doing.”

“Sill, no comment but spoiler alert: It’s getting warmer,” Sideris smiled.

“Well,” Hermine said. “I remember one of your rare public statements. Maybe it was a slip of the tongue back then but knowing you a little, I doubt it very much.”

“And what was that?”

“Well, in ‘02 you didn’t rule out aliens on our doorstep. Shortly after Distant Worlds One returned home and while Halsey was looking for her Architects of Creation you said some form of non-human sentience exists in our galaxy.”

“No, I said chances are that some form of non-human sentience exists in our galaxy. If you’re going to quote me, do it right.”

“Still, your comment struck me as odd back in the day. A bit too esoteric for the chairwoman of a megacorporation dealing with buying factual data. And in light of the discovery of the Guardian ruins just a few weeks later I just have to assume there was a PR clockwork ticking. Or maybe you already knew and needed a free of charge follow-up? A community goal, perhaps?”

“Only a fool would discard the notion of us being alone in this big galaxy. And what can I say? It turned out to be true, didn’t it?”

“What was the Guinevere doing out there? You don’t send out exploration cruisers with cryo crews on a whim. And why did we find the DSSA carrier near a permit locked sector? Were they looking for the Guardians?”

“Well, we kindly asked you to find out, didn’t we? But sadly, you seem to have failed in uncovering their whereabouts.”  

“So maybe they took all their secrets with them?”

“No Hermine. Quite the contrary. The plan still is to un-secret the secrets, so to speak. You see, I didn’t ask for this meeting just to show you my porcelain cup. I am going to make you an offer, and I’m only going to do it once. Because offers like these only come once in a lifetime.”

“Depends on how long you live,” Hermine muttered to herself.

“I recently spoke with Zanetti. He said the Pilots’ Federation Safety Board had registered certain ‘irregularities’ in some of your explorers’ ships firmware. Seems like someone inserted some datamining worms looking for extraordinarily exotic data. Something with spacetime and superdense masses?” 

The fox had just turned into a shark.

“I can’t remember all the details but it seems a very explorer thing to do?”  

Then Sideris put on her ‘I’m so sorry’ look. 

“I don’t think I need to point out that tampering with PF firmware is, shall we say, heavily frowned upon?”

Hermine decided to take it in strides. This was cleverly orchestrated and you best dealt with this kind of stunts level headed. Sideris alone was dangerous…

“The flotilla was exonerated by the three Powers, Cynthia. I am not too fussed or even, shall we say, intimidated by a mapping company that can’t even track their own vessels in charted space. You asked DSS to investigate while en route and this is what we did.” 

She made a short pause to let the slight sink in. Level headed, remember?

“If for some reason some of our independent entrepreneurs became creative in using their sophisticated onboard systems you can hardly blame an entire expedition for breaching the 23rd Amendment of the 3187 FTL Safety Act.”

“No, no. Of course not,” Sideris mused. “I am so relieved we are on the same page here and in fact this is entirely not why I am spending millions calling you face to face.”

“What then, Cynthia?” Hermine asked with an artificial sigh. “What are you spending your pocket money on?”

The projector made Sideris put aside her porcelain cup and lean forward on her couch.

“Like I said, I spoke with Zanetti and he put in a good word for you with the Board. So I am authorised to offer you a position within Universal Cartographics with a strong liaison to the PF. You would be Executive Director of a new department focusing on extreme range exploration. One of the key tasks would be to improve and innovate our technological capabilities in that field. It will become more and more important in the future to retain our corporate edge over rather uncoordinated civilian efforts.”

“Something you should be well acquainted with by cleverly manipulating your gone-rogue exploration amateurs,” she added with a (slight) glee.

“And of course,” Hermine concluded, “a few samples of that modified firmware and a debriefing on what we found out there would be beneficial for my nomination.”

Sideris’s smile just could not find an end.
