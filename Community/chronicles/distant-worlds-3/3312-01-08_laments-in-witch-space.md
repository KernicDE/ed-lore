NGC 1981 Sector ZU-Y d27, 3312 AD

The countdown rolled across every canopy like surf. On the main viewport the staging moon hung knife-bright, the armada strung about it in patient arcs, stars pinprick sharp in the backdrop.

The Halsey’s Quest’s bridge smelled of new plastics and warm metal, that particular mix ships have before they earn their grime. The Caspian was lean and purposeful with a forward gallery of glass looking out into the void. Conduits under the deck thrummed as the frame shift capacitors topped out, the FSD charge coil ticking as it bled heat into the keel.

“Wing leads green,” the comms officer reported. “Nav locks set. Wide-band clear. Mass locks show nominal.”

“Mass jump in five,” Captain Yuen said, not raising her voice. The sound carried anyway. “On my mark.”

Jasmina Halsey stood beside the rail with a borrowed headset and a view that would have made her twenty-year-old self weep. No Azalea to nudge her ribs with caution, no Cuthrick to fold a warning into a joke. They were back in Sol, shepherding the politics, holding the gates. She was here, a single presence wrapped in a new ship’s bones. The Quirium Control Unit sat in a foam cradle at her feet, sealed, inert, heavy as a thought.

“Three,” Yuen said.
“Two.”
“Punch.”

Frameshift pulled the stars to silk.

Witch-space opened, the corridor a familiar absence, the gentle throb of translation settling into the bones. The FSD’s phase coils found cadence against the tunnel wall, superluminal shear resolving to that almost-comforting bass roll every pilot learns to trust. Then the beat slid out of true. The corridor breathed in the wrong place. The auto-filter darkened the canopy against a flare that was not light as the bubble’s boundary shivered on the scopes.

“Hyperspace conduit integrity holding,” called the flight engineer, already leaning over a panel of ugly numbers. “Translation factor stable at point nine nine six.”

Jasmina did not need the recorder to tell her when it arrived. The pressure that haunted Olympus Village returned thicker, colder. A wash of not-sound crowded the edges of hearing. Words without language pressed towards her like a tide.

Grief.

“Low-band tick on harmonic grid,” the engineer muttered. “Fifteen again. Guardian booster notching but it is riding past the filter.”

“Shadow echo on wake three,” someone else said. “No object tracked. High-wake signature only.”

Open chatter bled through from wings off their bow.

“Anyone else seeing a reflection.”
“Ghost on the scopes. Fading.”
“I just heard… a voice. Like hearing your own name in a crowd.”

“Confirm faint modulation on ADC feed,” another voice added, professional and rattled. “Ship voice comms picking up cross-talk from the wake scanner. That is not supposed to happen.”

The conduit bucked once, corrected, then stuttered. Jasmina saw the HUD holofac flicker. A notch opened between beats as if a tooth slipped on a cog too large to see. In the notch something looked through her, not at the Halsey’s Quest but past it, the way weather fronts pass across oceans. Saturated with feeling. Tangled around a question it could not form. The Quirium unit at her feet gave the faintest pulse against the case foam like a cat butting a hand.

Jasmina closed her eyes. It made no difference. The corridor was inside her now.

You are not alone, she thought. We hear you.

The presence recoiled, not from her but from a pressure beyond them both. For a moment she sensed the rim of a larger shape, inevitability rather than malice. The consciousness folded itself small, like a hand snatching away from a flame it did not understand.

“Translation flicker,” said engineering. “Microdrop in containment at one part in ten thousand. AFMU is standing by. No faults logged.”

Normal space snapped back. A hard white star flooded the bridge. The Quest’s crew moved with that unshowy urgency good crews have, clearing boards, calling numbers, letting the heat sinks work instead of panicking them.

“Telemetry recorded,” said the engineer. “Clean capture on the trace. Wake scanner got a slice. Conduit-wall shear showed a blip.”

“Fifteen cycles exact,” comms confirmed. “Same cadence as Mars and Tionisla. Cross-checked against three hulls in our wing. Correlated, but the onboard chronometer desynced during transit. Updating now.”

Captain Yuen turned her chair half towards Halsey. “Madam Envoy?”

“Close enough to touch,” Jasmina said, steady because she had rehearsed being steady. “Grief and perplexity. It does not understand what is happening. It retreated.”

Yuen nodded once, as if someone had told her the weather would be foul on landing. “Log it. Stand by for supercruise.”

Hermine Delarue came up a breath later on the command frequency, silk over steel. “Science to fleet. If your recorder picked up harmonic distortion, tag and dump to DW3 archives. If your wake scanners flagged a cross-band echo, attach the raw. Voices on static, annotate the exact stamp from your flight recorder. Ghosts on scope, do not chase.”

Quartermaster Timotheus Haddard followed, his grin audible. “Logistics to fleet. If it felt weird, welcome to the tour. Refit windows open on the carrier from twenty to forty. If your AFMU threw a sulk after the jump get in line. Filter your complaints through science so I can blame our sponsors later.”

Jasmina muted the net and thumbed a private log. The words came clean.

She saved it and met the captain’s eye. “Captain Yuen, I will need a room with a map and two planners who hate surprises less than they say they do. And get Major Howard on comms. I hear she was at the Guardian ruins and hopefully has her logs from their scanning.”

The captain’s mouth gave the smallest ghost of a smile. “You will have it. Follow me.”

---
They took a glass-walled brief room immediately behind the bridge where the air still smelled of adhesive and fresh wiring. A star map unspooled along one wall. Two route planners hovered over it with the fussy tenderness of surgeons, overlays for scoopable stars and neutron superchargers already up. Hermine was there by telepresence, shoulders square, hair pinned by intent alone. Flight Major Elaine Howard had joined them on a secure line from her Whisky Wasp and Captain Yuen stood aside, looking at Jasmina.

“Show me the cadence,” Hermine said without preamble.

Jasmina pushed the packet. The display drew a clean band, then a bruise where the notch sat, then the smear that had made Martian engineers swear. Hermine dropped three anonymised traces on top. Different hulls, different drives, same wound in the rhythm.

“Voices on three,” Hermine said. “Ghost wake on seven. Two cases of ADC cross-talk. No pursuit, thank you. Correlation strongest in your wing, Envoy.”

“Fifteen on the miss again,“ Howard’s voice joined in. “We’ve seen that at the ruins, too, on the pico-hertz scale Ram Tah gave us. I’m going to have nightmares about that number. Any Thargoid signatures.” She waited a beat. “Good. Let us keep it that way.”

Fifteen…

Yuen folded her arms. “Madam Envoy, this is the same trace?”

“When we left Sol I could not find direction in it,” Jasmina said, with a nod. “Today there is a bias. Spinwise and coreward warms it. When I face the far northeast it sharpens like a tooth.’

Through her telepresence Hermine raised an eyebrow. “Interesting. I was recently told that region was called the Umbra Sagittarii once, the Archer’s Shadow. Basically, there be dragons. Fuel, spares, morale… it is a long way out. We do not peel an armada into the dark because a ghost asks nicely.”

“Who said that? The shadow I mean?” Jasmina demanded.

“One of our donors, although one of the odder ones. But he certainly knows a lot about early era space travel.” 

Howard literally could be heard rubbing her jaws over the comm. “DW3 already arcs that way. But we sure as hell didn’t gather three superpowers and a city of volunteers to ignore anomalies on day one. If we divert, how deep are you asking?”

“Far,” Jasmina said. “Where the survey routes thin to rumours.”

The planner glanced at the captain, then back to the map. “DW3 already trends spinwise and a touch coreward on the planned route. We can extend the reach without breaking the convoy. If we push a refinement branch we keep the route intact.”

“Options,” demanded the captain.

The senior planner traced a pale line with a fingertip. “We keep with the planned waypoints for now. From there we add a refinement: extra survey points step the convoy rimward and then back across. Fuel margins stay green based on K-class and scoopables on the leg. We can stitch in two neutron boosts to keep morale sweet and tanks fat.”

“Good,” Yuen said, then to Jasmina. “What do you think?”

“Yes,’ she said after a moment. ‘Feels right. The first step is almost on top of it. The second tells us if it sharpens or fades. The third is either the start of a map or proof we should not push. I should be able to triangulate as we proceed.”

“Science framing,” Hermine said. “We publish a dry note: anomalous witch-space signature detected, refinement added to collect comparative telemetry. No speculation. If you heard voices we need time stamps and raw. If you saw ghosts log, do not chase. If anyone writes the word ‘angel’ I will make them include a methods section and a double-blind.”

The captain looked to her comms officer. “Draft the fleet bulletin. Tag it Survey Refinement Alpha. Route planners lock the waypoints and push updates to every nav. Delarue you own the science packet. Howard you own the logs and cache timings. Everything goes through you. Madam Envoy…”

“I will ride the first leg and shut up unless the cadence moves,” Jasmina said.

“Good,” Yuen said, with a grin. “I like quiet envoys.”

The senior planner hesitated. “One more thing. If the anomaly links to jump distance we should vary the jump length a bit. Mix short and long transitions across the wing to see what resonates. We can also rotate Guardian FSD boosters in and out to see if the hybrid hardware changes the detection. Lots of variables to check.”

Hermine pointed. “Yes. Publish a pattern. Every third ship stretches one segment, every sixth compresses. Cross-index cadence against corridor length and Guardian hardware fit. Keep the variations small. I do not want cleverness.”

“Caspian hulls can take the long segment without sweating,” Howard said matter-of-factly. “I will tag three independents for the short hops. I have two pilots who fly like metronomes and one who thinks he is one. Probably Jacky Hew…”

“Add wake scanners to the lead elements,” the captain said. “If the corridor is bleeding into the scanner band I want it caught at source. Fit attenuation filters where you have them. No interdictions. We are not advertising a fight.”

“Mirrors to Alliance and Imperial science teams,” Hermine added. “Delayed, scrubbed, clean. No one cries foul later.”

“Do it,” the captain said.

---
The fleet bulletin dropped ten minutes later. A gentle chime rolled along the bridge. The comms officer read it once, then again, then nodded. “Survey Refinement Alpha published to all wings. Neutron boost brief added.”

Jasmina sent her field log to the DW3 buffer, a copy to Sol, one to Tionisla. Azalea’s reply arrived almost at once. Route accepted. Eat something. Sleep between legs. Cuthrick’s came a moment later. Your committees approve. I am bored already. Do something interesting and safe.

She smiled despite herself.

“Next mass jump in four,” Captain Yuen called, settling into her chair. “All wings report green. FSD charge at ninety-eight. Guardian boosters synced. Heatsinks cycled.”

The Halsey’s Quest’s bridge hushed in that way ships do when they listen to their own heartbeat. Outside, the armada slid into alignment, thousands of nav locks painting faint geometry across the dark.

Jasmina touched the rail, felt the paint still new under her fingers. The tug in her ribs was a thread now, not a pull, stretched toward the Archer’s Shadow. It did not feel like a destination. It felt like a question that had learned to wait.

“Wing leads green,” the comms officer said.
“Nav locks set. Wide-band clear.”
“Mass jump in five,” Yuen confirmed, the same even tone, the same weight. “On my mark.”

Jasmina steadied her breath. She had nothing to pray to so she counted, not the seconds but the beats of the drive, ready to catch the miss when it came.

“Three,” Yuen counted.
“Two.”
“Punch.”

Frameshift pulled the stars to silk. The corridor opened, the wake scanners sang in their leashes and the fleet leaned together into the dark.

Witch-space! We are coming!
