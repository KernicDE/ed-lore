NGC 1981 Sector ZU-Y d27, 3311 AD

She had wanted to take a day off to spend some time visiting the newest jewel of a world that had just been discovered in the Orion Cluster but a call from her ‘associate’ made sure that wasn’t an option anymore. So maybe another time…

Right now she sat in her spacious office, the Deep Space Surveys logo proudly displayed on the wall where at first a life support monitor had been. It had all been set up in a rush but that was okay. 

On her associate’s request she had invited one of his numerous contacts who now sat at the other side of the synth wood table. She had a job for him. He just needed to be motivated to accept it and that was exactly what her associate mentioned could be a problem — a test of wills so to say.

“Hermine Delarue. I work as an executive at Deep Space Surveys. Pleasure to meet you, sir.” She extended her hand over the desk, smiling her ‘let’s do business’ smile.

“Ah, Miss Delarue,” the man answered. “Finally. John speaks highly of you.”

“Who?”

He smiled gently. “Mister John then? The other ancient relic who always seems to be a bit gloomy and brooding? My size, the same grey hair, wears blue glasses?”

“Oh, you mean Mister Ermitage? Thank you, that’s reassuring. As a matter of fact he asked me to meet with you. So I trust you know each other well?”

He leaned back in the chair, apparently taking in the question. He kept smiling, but mostly to himself: “Just so, yes. We are what you may call very old friends.” 

She nodded at her still extended hand and the old man in the red and black suit carefully shook it, then withdrew again in an instant. “Charmed. You may call me Cal.”

She bowed her head slightly.

“And to what matters do I owe the invitation, Miss Delarue?”

She summoned two tumblers from an invisible drawer in her desk and reached for the bottle on her table, rich amber ‘94 Lavian Brandy. She started pouring the first glass and looked at her guest. “May I offer you one? It’s from a private selection.”

“No, thanks,” came the answer. “But please don’t let that deter you.” 

“Thank you. And truth be told, the matter is somewhat delicate,” she said while shifting uneasily in her chair. “Deep Space Surveys was approached by a group of higher ups in the Pilots Federation. They were looking for what they called experienced survey specialists with a decent track record.”

“And they came to you?”

“Thanks, charmer, but yes. We were a bit surprised as well. Although we do have a certain reputation in the business, we don’t often get inquiries from the premier league.”

“Oh, no offense to you and your little NGO,” he waved her off. “But as you say, the Pilots Federation have their trusted handlers for the matters you describe as ‘delicate’.”

“We assumed as much but after some digging it made sense to us.”

“Oh?” he quite obviously faked his surprise. “Care to elaborate?”

“Sure,” she answered. She took a sip from her tumbler and then leaned forward. “However, you need to promise me you will help with this matter first.”

“Need I?”

“Well, Mister Ermitage said you would be hesitant to provide help but should you consider it, he said you would have the opportunity to triumph in an old rivalry.” She shrugged. “Whatever that means.”

“Ha!” the old man burst out laughing. “At least you can see now John and I know each other. It is true not many things pique my interest these days and even fewer I would lend a hand to. In fact, it is the main reason your Mister Ermitage and I can be old friends. He has his ways… I had mine.’”

“Had?”

He seemed lost in thought for a moment before looking at her again: “How can I help you, Miss Delarue?”

She nodded: “Right. Over the last few weeks the Pilots Federation has collected data on an unusual number of starships failing to make contact at their scheduled destinations. Not commercial Bubble liners but deep space explorers. They also received a number of MIA reports filed by private citizens and a number of NGOs with urges to investigate further.”

“Did you ask them if they had contacted the chaps from DSSA or STAR?”

“Yes we did, and yes they did. According to the PF both organisations had no information on the missing ships and none of them had called port at their locations. Which would make it even weirder. The carrier networks out there are pretty sophisticated.”

“And why bother? As an executive of Deep Space Surveys you will be well aware that ships get lost all the time, especially the ones who are out there on their own. They all rely on their magical Field Units but most of them would struggle to tell the difference between a cohesion entangler and a classic screwdriver.”

She nodded. “Screwdrivers and privateers aside, I presume political or other influential circles may be involved. You are right, of course, the PF doesn’t consider themselves a rescue organization. I asked their representatives whether they would investigate.”

“That is interesting,” he mused, stroking his neatly trimmed chin beard. “What did they say? Oh and now I would definitely like one of your Lavian Brandys.” He pointed at the bottle.

Amused, Delarue took the bottle and the second tumbler, filled it with that glorious amber liquid, and poured herself another as well. So far, she thought, that test of wills was manageable.

“Well,” she continued. “Underneath all that polished corp gibberish, they fully realize they provide the infrastructure to keep space travel a thing but don’t usually lift a finger outside of their ‘newbie bubbles’.”

“Yes, ‘usually’,” he said while sipping on the Brandy. “But because your NGO is called Deep Space Surveys they thought to just ask you instead?”

“Not at first, no. But after some prodding as to why they approached us at all, they grudgingly conceded to Mister Ermitage that one of their state-of-the-art cartographics cruisers had gone missing, too.”

“Universal Cartographics? What an inconvenient turn of events. I am shocked!” He nonetheless chuckled. “Sideris must be fuming behind her desk. And they openly conceded that to you?”

“Like I said, only after Mister Ermitage dug a bit deeper. I don’t know how he did it and quite frankly, I don’t want to know. But he said he had called in a few favours and the Pilots Federation probably ran out of options. Our organizations are not at odds or in competition per se. It’s just that we have specialized equipment for a number of survey operations that we developed further after the construction of Explorers Anchorage.”

“Equipment that their state-of-the-art cruiser has not?” He chuckled again, this time slightly more fiendish than before.

“Don’t overthink it.” 

“No, it is just interesting,” he said, becoming all matter-of-factly again in an instant. “The Pilots Federation sits on a technological monopoly and together with their Dog Star pals they more or less dictate how space travel works. I thought with their modern firmware they are able to track a starship’s position through their fluid routers. I hear they run on entangled helium now.”

Delarue frowned. “They’ve been running on helium for 30 years?”

“See? What I said.”

“You’re an odd fellow, I have to admit. But Mister Ermitage warned me you might come across as… anachronistic. Well, warning is the wrong word. Let’s say he cautioned me that some of your answers would feel a bit… fallen out of time?”

“Did he now? That man flies a Lanner! And you are suggesting a number of exploration ships have gone missing out there without a trace and apparently with their non anachronistic firmware failing?”

“No. I don’t suggest that at all. It’s what the Pilots Federation’s reps told me. And yes, they asked me — or rather us — to investigate.”

“It is getting better and better. Where do they want you to investigate? Did they specify a region?”

“The data they provided marks most of the missing ships beyond the Core.”

He leaned back in his chair, toying with his glass of Bandy. “That is the Umbra Sagittarii,” he mused.

“Pardon me?”

“The Archer’s Shadow.” Sipping another sip, he continued: “Back in the day explorers thought the region beyond the Core was cursed. Off limits, so to say, because the thick interstellar clouds, stellar density and also the frequent energy bursts from the center made deep space navigation hazardous and unpredictable.”

“Back in the day? I’ve never heard that name before.”

“Most likely, because you weren’t born. Before the widespread release of the frameshift drive I only know of a handful of dedicated attempts to thoroughly chart these areas. The last one I know of came from the Alliance and it failed. They never heard from them again and according to their last broadcast they had barely made it beyond the Corona Australis.”

“I haven’t heard of that either.”

“Most likely, Miss Delarue, because your network of contacts ends somewhere. It was shortly after the Turners took over the Big Dipper. They were a bit shy about making it public at first, probably, because the Captain of that ill-fated expedition was a certain Hieronymus Halsey.”

“Halsey?” she asked in surprise? That name! There were a number of recent events around the current Federal Ambassador, Jasmina Halsey, but she hadn’t realised the family name ran all the way back to the infant Alliance of Independent Systems.

“The same,” Cal reinforced. “Did you know the Turners and the Halseys have a history? Captain Hieronymus Halsey was the great uncle of one of their best engineers. There had been rumors, of course,” he lectured. “People wondered back then how the Alliance was able to out-research long established powers like the Empire and the Federation. Some say it all came down to the disintegration of Galcop, the famous or infamous Galactic Cooperative. The rest they say is history…”  

Stunned, Delarue thought about the implications for a long moment. It all started to make sense — in a way. The troubling reports from Ermitage’s meeting with Ram Tah, the unannounced installation of a Galcop era sensor array on one of the Distant Worlds carriers, an expedition to the Guardian ruins, Jasmina Halsey’s recent statement on some cosmic peril, her strange trip to Tionisla; now the lost ships and Pilots Federation tech failing…

She took a deep breath: “I have to say I don’t understand half of what you’re saying but I begin to see why Mister Ermitage referred me to you. Your knowledge could prove very valuable.” 

“Miss Delarue, you have to understand I still fail to see how I can help you with this matter. The past is the past, the lost ships and their presumed locations are intriguing, the Pilots Federation apparently being in dire straits is refreshing, but what is it exactly that you want from me?”

‘Gotcha’, she thought. 

“You already answered that yourself, Cal: You seem to have deep insights about the unpredictables of space travel, especially hazards stemming from an earlier, less understood era. And you have a far reaching network of contacts. There may be things the Pilots Federation ignores, is unaware of or doesn’t tell us. Yes, they lost a top notch ship but do you really think that’s all there is to it?”

He smiled. “Ah, we are getting to the point now, are we not? Just say it then.”

She sighed: “We would be happy to have you as a consultant on shall we say ‘unannounced or unwanted involvements’.”

“Why?”

“Because we were asked by the Federal Astrocartography Department two days ago to accept a number of their vessels into the Distant Worlds flotilla. And while we are always open minded and willing to share exploration data with the galactic community, we of course also suspect it’s because they were kindly asked by the Pilots Federation to get involved.”

“What a daring suggestion.”

“Isn’t it?” she said with a wry smile. “However, the expedition needs to stay apolitical. I’m aware there are a lot of smaller squadrons on the roster, most of them unaffiliated and some of them pledged. That’s okay, we fully expected that. But the Federation ‘offering’ a number of their ships as a token of support is a new level. They want to name an envoy or a kind of liaison officer, which seems prudent and transparent…”

“...but you feel that is where your unwanted involvements could begin,” he ended the non-question.

“Precisely. Do you now see our predicament?” 

“Well, Miss Delarue,” he said, emptying the tumbler. “I am sure Mister Ermitage would agree that unwanted involvements are my specialty.”

Now it was her turn to smile: “He may have mentioned it…”, toasting him with her glass of Brandy.
