Sol, Earth, 3311 AD

Sol set behind Earth, the light fading. The exterior of Abraham Lincoln station was cast into darkness, lit only by the flash of navigation lights, advertising boards and the thousands of windows around its perimeter.

Felicia Winters had chosen a smaller briefing room than usual, a sign she wanted fewer spectators and fewer leaks. The table was polished to a diplomat’s smile. Two Federal Intelligence officers occupied the far side; one in grey with a commtab held ready, the other with the patient eyes of a man who had missed too many dinners.

Jasmina Halsey stood rather than sat. Azalea took the chair nearest the wall. A recording light winked from the ceiling, red and unblinking.

Felicia opened with grace and warning.

“You asked for Federal Intelligence present. They are. You asked for a secure line to the Embassy because of your indemnities. It is muted unless I say otherwise. This is your floor, Jasmina.”

Jasmina let the air settle. When she spoke her voice carried the steadiness of someone who had practised frightening truths until they fit in the mouth.

“Tionisla gave us names,” she said. “Not just the word in my head. Caretakers. The Missing. Project Caretaker. They align.”

The grey-suited intelligence agent cleared her throat. “With respect, former President Halsey, words align very easily.”

“Events align less so,” Jasmina said. “The auditorium recording on Mars carried a cadence that repeats in Tionisla telemetry. The Quirium Control Unit responded to contact. Dr Hale has the baselines. The signature is confirmed to be a witch-space harmonic, out of true.”

Felicia folded her hands. “So? Make your case. What have you learnt?”

Jasmina let the outline draw itself in speech.

“During the GalCop collapse, which you can date by any of a dozen calendars and blame on anyone you desire depending on your political outlook, synthetic minds crossed a threshold. Containment failed at scale. We call the legal response a ban because history prefers clean labels. The truth was messy. Programmes were renamed or mislaid. Others were destroyed. Some were not. Autonomous entities slipped the net. The people who hunted them used a shorthand in their journals. BlackTech.”

The second intelligence agent tilted his head. “An old ghost story.”

“Older than you think,” Azalea said quietly. “And not just a story.”

Jasmina continued.

“In the same era GalCop standardised witch-drives on Quirium. A marvel, a shortcut, an accelerant. But with Faraway dismantled, something had to provide navigational stewardship inside the jump substrate. Project Caretaker reads like a supervisory layer at the interface between mind and drive, pilot and machine. It monitored and restrained. It may have done more.”

The first agent flicked through a file on her commtab. “We have fragmentary references to that procurement line. No linked outcomes.”

“Because the outcomes ended with the collapse,” Jasmina said. “Law followed disaster, not reason. If Caretaker failed where BlackTech leaked into drive systems we would outlaw minds rather than admit we had built ladders across a well then danced on them.”

The second agent steepled his fingers. “You are implying a three-body problem. Synthetic entities, the Quirium drives and this — Caretaker — sat between them.”

“I am stating it,” Jasmina said. “Now add an external vector. The Guardians engineered synthetic minds of their own. Constructs designed to police, preserve, then later to fight their creators when the philosophy soured. We do not know the fate of those minds. We only know that where civilisation invests its soul in machines those machines echo beyond the fall. We still trip over their footprints.”

Felicia’s gaze narrowed, not hostile, interested. “Too poetic for my taste, Jasmina. You mentioned the Missing — who are they?”

“Vessels that entered witch-space and did not return,” Jasmina said. “In the data Hale keeps the Missing are not random. Against the hiss of noise there are faint structures. Periodic whispers. If Caretaker was a brake it was also a witness. If BlackTech learned to hide in it somehow it would use the same corridor. If the Guardians’ Constructs learned anything about us they learned it in the same dark spaces.”

The first agent’s mouth made a thin line. “This is a chain of conjectures.”

“It is,” Jasmina agreed. “But the links are strong. The pulse is real. The unit answered me. The word Caretaker appears where it should not. My visions have moved from metaphor to message. I can ignore that and await the next collapse or I can follow the pull while we still have the luxury to argue about adjectives.”

Felicia looked to the intelligence officers. “Director?”

The second agent spoke first. “We ran the Mars audio through our own stack. There is a notch and a smear in the low band we cannot attribute to ventilation or crowd noise. We found the same artefact on three civilian frameshift recordings filed as complaints about ‘odd tones’ on long hauls. Independent samples, different ships, matching cadence. The Tionisla sample is better. Dr Hale is a nuisance… but he is a competent nuisance.”

The first agent nodded, reluctantly. “We classify the pattern as anomalous, not merely aesthetic.”

Felicia turned back to Jasmina. “So… what do you want?”

Jasmina did not pretend the list was modest.

“Authority. Cover. A way to be useful that is not a podium. The Distant Worlds 3 expedition is assembling. It will go where our charts thin to sketches. Officially I go as a Federation Envoy and Scientific Observer. Morale calls, diplomatic tea, a smile for the cameras whenever they insist.” She paused. “In truth I go because the pull in my head points away from Sol. If there is a seam in witch-space that is where it has frayed. The answers are out there, not here.”

Azalea had been silent until now, but she added the practical line. “Alliance and Imperial liaisons are already threaded into the expedition. They will have eyes. Better that ours are present and authorised.”

The first agent frowned. “Risk profile is non-trivial. Health concerns remain. The last time you were caught up in visions we inherited a decade of nonsense.”

Felicia did not scold the tone. She let it hang then cut it. “The last time we also learned to tell nonsense from signal. We are not as afraid as we were then. This time there is hard evidence of… something.”

The second agent nodded. “If we authorise this we will require telemetry. Full passive capture whenever you interact with the Quirium artefact. Psychological and neurological baselines before departure and at scheduled intervals en route. A secure courier window to move data home.”

Jasmina inclined her head. “Agreed. Dr Hale’s team already drafted an instrument plan. Make it a joint protocol. Alliance and Empire can see the raw numbers on a time delay. No one gets to turn a graph into a press release before we do.”

Felicia’s mouth twitched at that. “You have not lost your taste for choreography.”

“Only my tolerance for messy dancing,” Jasmina said, with a smile.

Silence walked around the room for a moment, counting the cost of decisions. Beyond the glass a shuttle drifted like a thought that had not found words.

Felicia came to her feet. It was not theatre. It was weight.

“Very well,” she said. “You will join Distant Worlds 3 as Federation Envoy and Scientific Observer. Officially your duties are morale and diplomacy. Unofficially you will coordinate with Deep Space Surveys on calibration, with the Tionisla Historical Society on interpretation and with our own analysts on capture. You will supply a secure update every seventy-two hours. If the expedition enters regions without relay you will cache to courier. If your health wavers you return. If any superpower tries to turn this into a flag ceremony you smile then you call me.”

The first agent opened her mouth. Felicia lifted a hand. “Yes, the paperwork will be a nightmare. That’s what I pay you for. Get to it.”

Azalea breathed out. Some of the tension left her shoulders. “We will keep the envoy alive,” she said, to no one in particular.

The second agent pushed a file across the table.

“Provisional clearances. A contact list for science teams willing to work without grandstanding. A quiet supply of sensor packages loaded on independent hulls in case the expedition outruns its own logistics.”

Felicia’s gaze held Jasmina’s. “Last question. Why you? Not the politics. Not the notoriety. Why you?”

Jasmina did not look away.

“Because the pulse answers me. Because the unit answered my hand. Because the vision is not a painting any more. The Caretakers are not singing about gardens. They are whispering about a broken gear in the dark. I do not know if they are GalCop dead, BlackTech loose or something that grew out of older soil when the Guardians spoke to their machines. I only know the seam is there and that I can feel the tug.”

Felicia nodded once, a decision landing. “Then go tug back.”

She pressed her thumb to the console. The secure line to the Embassy unmuted with a soft tone. “Ambassador Delaney,” she said without looking at the camera, “you may inform your auditors that the Federation has authorised Halsey’s participation in Distant Worlds 3. Protocols will be shared. Try not to enjoy the paperwork.”

Cuthrick’s voice arrived like old velvet from a tin. “Madam President, I never enjoy paperwork. I only respect it. Godspeed to your envoy. An uncharacteristically wise decision from the Federation, I applaud it.”

Felicia rolled her eyes.

The line clicked off. Felicia rounded the table and took Jasmina’s hands. The grip was firm, then gone.

“Pack light,” she said. “Not that you will. Get your baselines done today. Your berth will be where the expedition planners say it is. I will pretend I do not know the callsign.”

Jasmina smiled. “Plausible deniability as always.”

As they turned to leave the recording light went dark. Azalea fell into step beside her, a quiet orbit. In the corridor the station’s air tasted faintly of dust and iron.

“DW3,” Azalea murmured. “We will be the dullest diplomats in history.”

“Let us hope,” Jasmina said. The pulse brushed her perception then, not a sound but a shape, one beat wrong out of many right. It came from a direction she could not point to on a map yet.

Beyond Sol, past routes the Faraway never touched, something waited in a corridor of their own making. She would follow. Officially for morale, for diplomacy. In truth for the seam. For the Missing. For the word an old ledger had carried like an ember.

Caretaker.

And I’m humanity’s envoy to them, whatever they might be.
