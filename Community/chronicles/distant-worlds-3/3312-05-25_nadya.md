Distant Worlds 3 HQ, 3312 AD

She had started packing the moment they arrived in the Orion Bubble and the expedition was over. Her Asp was already on standby and her blue overall and her service cap lay neatly folded on her bunk.

"Leaving us so soon, are you?" she heard that familiar, old voice behind her.

She turned around while binding her ponytail into a short knot.

"There is still the matter of the leak," she insisted. "And the fact Aegis was allowed to the Three Powers summit. I find it concerning, John. Concerning enough to investigate."

"So Nadya is not heading back for Serebrov?"

"No. Apart from the leak and the summit there is still the matter with the Sadr Region."

"And what is that?" John asked.

"It struck me as odd that Hewley Jackman knew about Quirium drives as well; at least superficially. When we investigated the prototype drive of the DSSA Carrier he said he had seen something similar in what he called a 'lone science station in the Sadr region'. I think it is worthwhile to check his background and I intend to inform the Sadr cell."

"I was surprised that anybody knew anything about Quirium at all. GalCop collapsed a long time ago and Falayn went awol. And in Tionisla those control rods are revered as holy artifacts of a bygone age. However, I was not surprised that what little knowledge was left was put into a prototype carrier's hyperdrive. You probably did good to dismantle it when you had the chance. Some secrets deserve to be lost."

She seemed lost in thought for a moment. "I could sense Jacky's anxiousness in that conference and I could not stop pondering. I think he wanted to say more. After all, he always does, but at that moment he refrained."

"That is why I will meet him again," she added. "Although quite likely he will not expect my visit."

He looked at her for a small eternity.

"Very perceptive. And yes, you were always excellent at sensing or reading other people's feelings. It's a gift, really, and you have used it extensively to gain an edge over your assignments. Hell, you even managed to piss off Halsey. That's quite an achievement."

"She is arrogant," she insisted.

He paused.

"You're wrong, Mir. She really isn't. She's brusque, yes. But she's also wounded by the truth. When we speak about personas, don't forget hers was annihilated by her political adversaries after having miraculously survived a covert assassination attempt. And her survival is owed to what was found during Distant Worlds. That's huge. The truth is she was right from the beginning but was sent to a looney bin for it. People should have listened instead."

"I do not think it is over, John. When we surveyed the Norma Arm ruins, the synchronously emitted light patterns of the obelisks suggested that her connection is still active."

"Mir," he said with a soft smile. "I'm not going back to Serebrov either. I can't. There is only that stasis tank waiting for me and who knows when they will unfreeze me again and tell me the galaxy needs saving."

"It's time," he added.

"Time for what?"

"Time to see an old, trusted companion, and then it's time to go."

She turned away abruptly, starting to casually wrap a needle pistol in a towel and stowed it in her bag along with her other few belongings.

"All the more reason to be on my way then," she insisted.

"As Nadya?"

"That is not an option John. I need to adjust her ID and pick a new persona. Procedure requires it. There will be inevitable inconsistencies if someone starts checking her background and we cannot risk that. A few people already started to take an interest during the expedition."

"Well yes, irritating Halsey may have gained you some sympathy," John laughed.

"That is not who I meant and you know it."

"Yes of course, but allow me some humour for a change. I realize meeting Alessia and Andrew again must have been awkward."

"No, I kept it professional. And so did they, as best as they could. Both of them emphasized they have moved on, whatever that means."

"And you know both of them are lying to themselves."

"Sure, but it was not them either. One day when I went to work on the refueling schedule I found a queue of pilots who wanted to thank me for managing their payload intakes."

"And?"

"It was awkward. They came very close."

"Well, they must have been really confused when they found out you don't shake hands."

"I remember you saying it's dangerous for an Elite to have friends," she said with her back still towards him. "How there is always someone out there trying to get you, how friends are a liability and how someone will always try to use them against you. It was part of my training to not have friends and to utilise these liabilities against our adversaries."

"I was bitter and angry," he softly conceded. "Mostly with myself. There will always be more adversaries than friends, Mir, and I came to realise it's why friends are so important. This expedition showed me the value of camaraderie, but it also made painfully clear that I belong to a time that is over."

"And that is it?" she asked, turning half around again. "You have been there from the beginning, right from the start when the Formidine Rift was only known to a few and only whispered among the foolhardy. Those were your words back then. And now the Founder is leaving behind what he founded?"

"It's a stupid title, given to me by the folks who occasionally unfreeze me and ask equally stupid questions. I never liked that. Yes, I had an idea a long time ago how the galaxy could be a better place but nearly all my high and mighty plans failed. And after a small eternity in torpor in this stasis tank I realised it was all just pretence; a redemption arc for the things I did — the things they made me do."

"What I really founded," he added, "was an egoistical plea for absolution. And there is no glory in that. Nor is there any salvation, pun intended."

He sighed. "I only have one friend left and she rests on bedrock. I need to see her one last time and then the galaxy will have its peace with one ancient anachronism gone."

"There is still the other," Mir mused.

"He has his own demons. And he knows they will eventually catch up with him. There is no need to hunt him down now. And he's possibly one of the few people who will elude you anyway if he so wishes."

"He did indeed. Despite you asking me to shadow him I was not very successful."

"That's not true. I knew the odds but I had to make certain he felt shadowed. It's our way of playing chess."

"He is an apparition," John added. "Very much like I am one. But you, Child, you need to care for the living."

"Care?"

"You'll figure it out." He smiled.
