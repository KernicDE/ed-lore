Thuecheae MT-Q e5-8 System, 3312 AD

The Seldowitch Nebula hung like a bruise in the darkness, a scatter of red along its rim where young stars bit through the dust. Distant World explorers swarmed the area. Mapping drones went out. Out here, there were dragons.

Mineral teams took polite bites of rings. The bulk synthesizers that kept the limpets coming were always hungry. Science kept its lists. Jasmina played her part on the briefings and then stepped off the stage, because behind the public cadence the flotilla had other work. And because Azalea scolded her to eat and sleep. On occasion.

Lya’s map, based on the corrupted cone and that ancient satellite’s broken voice, gave the search and rescue detachment a thread to follow. They slipped spin-wise and up between scheduled survey legs, flying quiet. The space around was remarkably unremarkable. Why were parts of the area locked down? And what did ‘Praei’ even mean? An illusion of normality was just about to insert itself into…

“Cold mass, carrier scale,” a certain Commander LilacLight radioed in from afar. From somewhere. “No transponder. No power. Drifting on a lazy vector toward the Praei-two plane. Looks like we're at the boundary of that permit lock. No in betweens.”

The image that came back filled the glass of one of the Odysseus’ holo-walls: A lone planet hung below a somewhat ordinary star. In its grasp… a carrier? But… not. Bigger along the spine than most, broad, modular, with parts strangely resembling the recent Squadron class carriers; but with odd bulges where docks should be. No callsign. No livery. No lights.

“Prototype,” Elaine Howard muttered. “I recognize some Brewer modules maybe. Someone built a secret and forgot to paint the name?”

“Locator team, passive scans only,” Hermine directed. “Don’t wake it up. No telling what the hell is going on here. Be careful out there, we’re moving in.”

---
Caution gave way to boredom, the carrier seemed utterly inert. Hermine eventually conceded they needed to find out more. She pulled up the duty roster: Elaine Howard and Hewley Jackman.

“Oh… this is going to be fun,” Hermine said, shaking her head in amusement and tapping in the summons.

Elaine arrived on the boarding bay already zipped into an old Alliance Scouting and Exploration Wing jacket, rank flashes still stitched where the regs say they belong. Four Purple Starburst ribbons glinted on the breast like knife-cuts that had learned to shine. Someone asked her once why she still wore it. “History,” she had said. That was that.

“Approach it,” Hermine said. “Passive scans first. If it sneezes, back out. We don’t know why this ship is here, or what it was doing…”

Jacky Hew, HewHew to his drinking friends, rolled a shoulder as the maintenance crew ambled past. “If you’d de-rate orbital cruise by a tenth you’d bleed less heat on approach,” he drawled.

“You will not preach orbitals in front of my people,” Elaine said, not looking at him. “You will hold a clean stack and you will not crash until after I’m finished with my ship.”

Hew grinned. “Copy that sir, Flight Major, sir!”

She didn’t correct him, just gritted her teeth. History.

“Just take Jasmina across, survey the ship, see if you can find out what was going on over there,” Hermine said. “We need some answers. Tell our Commanders to prep likewise. Second wave.”

Elaine looked at her, across at Jasmina, who gave a small nod. Elain then looked back to Jacky with a glare.

“Let’s go then, we’ve got a job to do.”

---
They went in through a maintenance lock that had jammed open. Jasmina was with them, despite their concerns. “It’s the Quirium,” she had insisted. Silent nods and no complaints. 

The inner skin of that carrier told a story of haste and bad choices. Bulkheads patched with plastisteel. Storage racks welded where designers never meant them. A tang in the air like hot plastic and stressed electronics.

“What a mess,” Jacky muttered.

“Lights to low,” Elaine ordered, the kind of tone that makes a panicked room breathe. “No touching anything that wants to be touched.”

They were twenty metres into the spine when the first filament lifted from the deck. The width of a finger, the length of a life, translucent and threaded with glitter like dust caught in a beam.

“What the fuck is that?” Jacky demanded.

“Hold it,” Elaine said. Everyone held still.

Jasmina tasted a metal tang in the air. The sealed Quirium Control Unit in the case at her hip shivered against her side as if remembering a rhythm she had tried to forget.

“Something?” Elaine asked, not taking her eyes off the slowly waving filament.

“It’s reacting,” Jasmina said. “Either the ship itself, or that thing…”

They eased past the filament, which didn’t further react to their presence.

They found the drive section two levels down. The chamber still smelled of tritium handling, an ozone tang mixed with machine oil, but the banks that should have held the fuel were empty. Converters along the aft bulkhead showed slag where heat had climbed beyond design and then stayed there. At the heart of it a gimballed cradle carried a lattice of gold mesh and dull ceramic wired to the reactor with an elegance that had no right to live beside angry welds.

“Hybrid rig,” Elaine breathed. “Primary tritium feed, secondary… I’ve not seen anything like this before...”

“I have, on a lone science station in the Sadr Region,” Jacky said, the smugness clear in his voice.

“And?” Elaine demanded.

He took his time, clicking his tongue and licking his lips audibly.

“Just say it,” Jasmina said.

“That’s pretty close to GalCop tech,” he replied. “There was this stuff called Quirium,” he nodded towards Jasmina and her device. “One geek out there thought he had it all together but blew himself up. I saw his crazy matrix once when I borrowed some synth materials from him.”

They stopped and listened.

“What? You think old Jacky and his yarn? I may be crazy but guess what.” He pointed to the gold lattice. “Looks like plasma coils. Pretty much like in Sadr. Old school. Not fuel lines. Some kind of control lattice. Like they meant to shape a field with Quirium the way old witch-drives did. But there’s no Quirium here. Nothing in tanks. Nothing in residue. They ran on tritium till the converters cooked. Then boom…”

Jasmina’s case thrummed like a trapped heart. She set it out on the deck plating. The throb rose, missed on fifteen, rose again. Filaments along the bulkhead turned their ghostly tips as if listening.

“OK… this is creeping me out,” Elaine said, soft and absolute, before pointing at Jasmina’s case. “Is that thing dangerous? We should get footage for Nadya Holdstock.”

“Not so far,” Jasmina said. “But it’s definitely reacting to these filaments. And yes, use your bodycams.”

The corridors were worse on the way back. The filaments had found friends. Sheets of the same glistening substance hung like wet banners between racks. Where they pooled they turned glossy and granular, a skin of polymer with a shimmer that made the eye slide away.

“What the actual fuck!” Elaine stammered.

They found the first body at a junction. Or what had been a body. The suit had slumped, then flowered into a framework of grey protrusions, struts and fingers that had no business being based on a human form. A faceplate peered from a web the way a seed looks out from a pomegranate.

Jacky took a scan.

“Nanite compounds,” he said, voice unusually serene and tight. “Not medical. Looks industrial. Partially decomposed… partially… disassociated and reformed into new chemicals.”

What had been an outstretched arm was now mangled, partially altered in form, looking almost like a badly designed mechanical grip manipulator.

“Do not touch,” Elaine snapped. She angled her camera and told herself she would not remember this in her sleep.

“What could do this?” Jasmina asked.

Jacky shook his head. “They were messing about with some weird shit onboard, that’s all I can tell you…”

They cleared the command level after an hour of cold caution. The primary bridge was dead. Consoles showed a last logon screen then garbled data. The secondary bridge still had power to a few spines, just enough for light. There were no more bodies, but in places there were patches of the grey substance sticking to walls and corridors. It was matt and featureless, eerie, but inert. They could see the remnants of suits, RemLok masks and uniforms embedded in it.

“Most of the crew didn’t get away,” Jasmina said, swallowing. “They’re still here…”

“Here,” Elaine said, jabbing at a console.

The logbook was incomplete. Earlier entries were neat, then hurried, then angry. The last entry was typed in a rush.

The line stopped as if the hand had been pulled away by someone rude to endings.

“Evac where?” Jasmina asked over Elaine’s shoulder. “Some did escape? Where did they go?”

“Flight deck,” Elaine said while downloading the logs. “If they launched that T-9, the answers would be down in the bay in the launch protocols.”

The bay had been purged. The interior was scorched and tarnished. Clamps showed marks where something big had slept then woken, burning out at full throttle and boost. The air smelled clean in a way the rest of the ship did not.

“Maybe they got out before the goo caught up with them?” Jacky asked.

“Maybe,” Elaine said.

Jasmina shivered. This wasn’t the answer, neither was it the question. But this grey goo was involved. She knew that for sure.

---
They pulled back to the Caspian following a thorough decontamination routine. Hermine took the lead with the patience of someone who likes plans more than surprises. The engineering teams were already pouring over the data from the log and the scans. Nadya Holdstock, their fuel wizard was present, too, her ponytail tucked away under her service cap as usual.

“We can leave it to drift and hope it meets a star,” Hermine said. “It’s a graveyard based on what you’ve discovered. DSSA doesn’t seem to care anyway.”

Jasmina nodded, unable to form words.

“Quirium…,” Jacky said ominously. “Converters burnt out. Tritium feed is repairable if we strip a tug. We can bring it to idle with enough rods. If you want…?”

“You and your Quirium. Another yarn tale?” Hermine shook her head.

“I don’t think we should touch it again,” she continued, eyeing Jasmina’s case as if it might answer back. “Whatever they were doing got out of control… same might happen to us. Last thing we need is some weird nanite epidemic spreading across the fleet. And actual Quirium fuel? In 3312? What does this even mean?”

At that point, Nadya Holdstock cleared her throat: ”Doctor Falayn’s fuel was lost long ago. But it was lost in the upheaval that was caused by the disintegration of GalCop. So we have a recurring pattern based on ancient tech, which may be more important than the fuel itself. It was a prototype and apparently failed dramatically. So we can’t use it.”

She looked at Jasmina: “But add to this your curious Quirium Control Unit from the depths of the Tionisla archives, the Fimbulthul’s GalCop deep space sensor array that was apparently ‘borrowed’ from the Sarasvati and the Faraway era satellite the DSSA carrier used as emergency comms beacon.”

“What are you implying?” Hermine asked.

“Nothing, just that there is a pattern of technology that today is poorly understood and exceedingly difficult to replicate.”

Elaine stood with the bagged log in her hand, jaw set. “Let’s focus. There is a Mayday and we’re still missing people in a T-9,“ she said. 

“We have no clue where they went,” Captain Yuen interrupted. “If that’s all you could pull from the logs, even with the current stellar density, it’s the proverbial haystack.“

“A science team left in a hurry and we don’t know where,” Elaine said. “But if they’re alive they’ll have answers. If they’re dead they might have logged something useful. This GalCop stuff is getting weirder and weirder and we need to get to the bottom of this.”

“And you’re ex Alliance,” Jacky laughed. “You bought them out, remember?”

“Ha, ha, ha, jackass!”

“Elaine is right,” Jasmina said. “We must either find them or ask around if they turned up somewhere. We’re in a better position for the latter at this point.”

Hermine nodded. “DSSA?”

“Yes. I feel they owe us some answers anyway.”

“Orders?” Elaine asked impatiently.

“Quarantine cordons everywhere,” Captain Yuen said. “Any reports of anything weird and we clamp down hard. Pull samples for Lya under burn boxes. Then more scanning.”

Jasmina looked at the dark shape on the main screen. A nameless carrier with a nameless hunger in its bones. The sealed Quirium unit at her hip ticked once.

“Send to the DSSA Council,” Hermine told comms. “Contact made with an unmarked carrier adrift near the Praei-two plane. Prototype drive with hybrid architecture. No power. No crew. Strange residue onboard. Quarantine in force. We’ll leave a warning beacon alongside advising visitors to give it a wide berth.”

“And the evac craft?” Elaine asked.

“Note an auxiliary craft is out there somewhere, probably a jury-rigged T-9,” Hermine said. She swallowed. “Tell them the truth: location and vector unknown. Not sure they made it, the logs mention an unstable reactor.”

Jasmina watched the preparations and the orders. There was a slight sense of relief, but also foreboding. A premonition. She was beginning to understand… but there was much more left to learn.

We’re on the right track… but where does it lead?
