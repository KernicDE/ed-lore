DW3 Expedition Command, Veils Region, 3312 AD

The data didn’t just trickle in. It nearly overloaded Fimbulthul’s already vast databanks and barely left any processing power for modelling the intake into a cohesive something. The thousand or so neutron stars that had been scanned by expedition Commanders over the last week provided a much needed insight into what they were actually tracking out here. 

In the one or two days that followed the galaxy spanning telepresence team had managed to puzzle a few pieces together. At one time they even included Jaques back in Colonia. And although most of the time he had limited himself to an “ah yes, that one” or a “this is probably as accurate as it gets”, his knowledge of old technologies on the other hand had come in more than handy. 

So with all that data Lya and Ram Tah had been able to come up with a simulation that could best be described as a fine network or lattice of filaments connecting ultra dense masses. And what was perplexing: This network also connected to locations with extreme hyperspace entry telemetry — their so-called mass jumps — and places that for lack of a better term could only be described as anomalies — points of interest that didn’t fit into one of the prior categories. Purpose unknown or unconfirmed. It was a gigantic spider’s web laid onto the Milky Way. It was absurd to not think a hidden purpose lay behind all this. Caretakers, architects…

Interestingly, Colonia was one of these anomalies. The Guardian ruins in the Synuefe and Wregoe sectors near the Bubble were others. And Tionisla of all places. There were a few more and some of them were quite concerning. Some were very concerning. Some of them were permit locked. 

“There!” a holographic Lya pointed her finger right into the equally holographic display they were all sharing. “The models don’t lie. This… this lattice is real. I mean in the sense that it exists and can be measured. And on top of that it shows we ‘Temps’ may have a point with our highly unconventional zero time continuum thingie after all.”

Across the telepresence projector and quite a few light years between them she shot Ram Tah a flirtatious smile. “Nice work, Mister Engineer. When I’m back home I need to juggle a few more dimensions in my comfortable maths equations and see where that gets us.”

Ram’s holographic brows formed their characteristic ‘V’.

“Aye and thanks. But you are home, aren’t you? This is just… telepresence?”

“Oh I know! I mean when DW3 is over. I need to publish this once the models are tried and tested. Don’t worry, Ram, you’ll be credited.”

At that point Jasmina Halsey interrupted. 

“Not so fast with our galactic horses. We need to put it all to work here. We have unfinished business with our friends in witch-space and some AI that tries to reconfigure matter for reasons unknown. This is not for academic publication but for fleet communication. Any guesses? We need to tell our 8,000 Commanders something.”

“Why?” Lya asked.

Jasmina shifted from one foot to the other, a rare sign of nervosity.

“When people don’t have something to do, the same people start to talk. Just ask Jacky. There are a number of mess hall discussions around any number of topics. Gossip mostly, but some of these talks always circle around the things we saw and what we found out here. The inexplicable bits. And when I say ‘out here’ I mean in this remoteness of space. People get anxious when they can’t place events or find explanations for the things they see. You can try and divert their attention with hydroponic gardens and a few stratum techtonicas but ultimately they will start making connections themselves, some of them nearly always turning out to be unhealthy.”

“So what do you suggest?” the Engineer asked.

“I suggest finally making good use of that GalCop wizardry on the Fimbulthul. They took elaborate efforts to install, nay, jury-rigg these sensor array relics onto the carrier and they are supposed to help in deep space surveys. Well, we are as deep in deep space as it gets and this Mister Ermitage seemed quite certain it would be of good use when the time comes. And I think that time has finally come.”

“We don’t know if it’s even working,” Ram mused. “Ermitage was pretty vague about its capabilities — let alone its origin.”

“Well,” Jasmina said, “let’s see if it works at all. And if it doesn’t we can still pull the plug.”

“That’s what I thought back in my workshop, too, not so long ago. It’s easier to contain a planetside base than a flotilla in a non accessible area of cold vacuum.”

“I agree. Turning one of our carriers into a flying listening post isn’t what I had in mind when I joined. But we have tonnes of unfinished, raw data and limited options. The array, however old and odd it may seem, was meant to augment our surveys and that’s what we’re going to use it for now.”

“Jasmina,” Lya said. “That’s all and well but can I recommend to sever all non essential connections to our other science operations for the time being? Like Ram, I don’t fully trust this technology no matter how sophisticated it may seem. With a stressing on ‘seem’.”

“Noted,” was all Jasmina had to say to this.

First it was an anomaly, a shiver in their filament, but it was pointing somewhere promising: a neutron star in a bright supernova remnant. Because of its location and colors earlier explorers had dubbed it the Veil’s Ember. It was one of the more prominent locations in this remote area of the Milky Way so the team was surprised to have it show up on the diagrams at all.

They had managed to give the lattice a visual form in all their simulations. Right now it was like a thin fabric laid on a liquid surface that was the galaxy. It came complete with ripples and eddies and around the Ember remnant the holoprojector showed a blurred eddie, maybe some fifty light years in diameter. It was… science beautified.

“There,” Lya said. “We found it roughly two hours ago. It’s a strong signal and it’s singing in perfect harmony with your lattice signature and with what we would call a density event. Mathematically speaking it’s a perfect equation. We can’t pinpoint its exact location, though, the model isn’t refined enough.” 

“Singing?” Jasmina frowned

“A curious metaphor, I know.”

“No, not at all. I sometimes wake up in the middle of the night thinking I hear a choir out there singing in witch-space. All these visions and voices, they more and more blend into a melody of sorts. It’s strange but it always… soothes me.”

“You mentioned a lot of people see or hear eerie stuff in your flotilla. That’s why I don’t like space travelling, I guess. There are just too many unknown variables floating around and some of them now seem to have a life and voice of their own.”

“So that GalCop array works?” Jasmina abruptly asked, looking left.

At her side, Nadya Holdstock nodded. She absently brushed a strain of her blonde hair aside and cleared her throat.

“Yes, it does, at least according to the specifications from Mister Ermitage. He gave some instructions on how to online the array to the Fimbulthul’s sensor dishes and so far there have been no incidents of note.”

“Of note?” Jasmina shot.

Nadya just looked at her, her face expressionless.

“So he gave you the specs in advance?”

“Of course.”

“What aren’t you telling me then? You and Ermitage? You worked together putting the logistics in place back at DW3 HQ. You know him personally, do you?”

“You know that already so why the question?”

“How long? And what are you even to him? Just a coincidental Quirium tech? Where does all this secret knowledge come from? Is he in league with this ‘Cal’ guy?”

“And I ask: Does it matter here and now? He apparently knows what he is doing and although I doubt he could have foreseen the exact odds we are facing, his insight into old technology has served this expedition well.”

“That’s not what I asked.”

“But this is my answer to your question. And yes, he is what you call in league but not in the sense you think he is. He is also in league with you, Halsey; and with Delarue and in fact with most of the hirings for this endeavour. Now, do you want us to find out more about this singing anomaly or do you want to persist in futile questions?”

“How dare you…”

“Guys!” Lya pleaded over a thousand light years. “We have a space anomaly and Jasmina, you want to tell 8,000 people what to do. Guess it’s not the time to tell them to go explore family trees, no?”

Jasmina hesitated for a good moment, apparently wrestling with her composure. One final glare at Nadya, but finally responsibility won over.

“Good. Let’s go sightseeing then. Tell Jacky to take out his Krait. I want him around when we find out more. Elaine will stay with me on the Odysseus, coordinating things from here.” 

---
The anomaly turned from a shiver in the fabric into a solid wreckage; another one. Intrepid Commanders found it near the Ember in a somewhat unremarkable system. Jacky Hew was still out with them. It was unusual the wreckage had lain hidden for so long but the multitude of stellar bodies in the system along with some probable earlier explorer’s space boredom may have been parts of the reason. 

The wreckage itself was huge, bigger than the hapless T-9 they found near the Seldowitch Nebula a month ago. It was an Anaconda-class vessel, half buried in the moon’s regolith, half broken with parts strewn around. It wasn’t long before SRV teams were on site, trying to uncover the ship’s fate.

Meanwhile, back at the carrier Jasmina and Hermine Delarue had quickly assembled their usual debrief team, including Nadya Holdstock and also Captain Yuen from the Halsey’s Quest. The Caspian was already on pre-flight checks.

“Right, people,” Jasmina began the debriefing. “I asked Nadya and Jacky to go over the footage and data we received from the wreckage. Jacky, you’re still out there, you need to keep them on a leash for the time being and don’t let them do anything stupid. You know, SRV jumping over the ‘Conda, paintballing themselves or something similarly ridiculous.”

“Righto, Ridiculousness is one of my major talents, my Lady.”

“Yeah, right,” Hermine mused.

“Okay, team. There are several Commanders out there now waiting for some feedback so let’s throw together what we have. Nadya?”

With a stern expression Jasmina looked at the technician. She clearly hadn’t forgotten the barbs traded earlier.

“The wreckage is interesting,” Nadya stated matter-of-factly. “I was able to analyse most of the data explorers have gathered and also parts of the hull itself.”

“Not bad for a fuel tech, eh?” Jacky Hew jabbed over telepresence from his Krait.

Nadya just shot him a glare through the projector that immediately shut him up, which was rare enough.

“Right, ahem…” was all he could mutter.

“It is an Anaconda, yes,” she continued. “And it bears the same hull disintegration we have seen in the DSSA carrier and its evacuated T-9. That much is certain but that is not all.”

“What do you mean?” Hermine asked.

“Despite its sorry condition there are some incongruities I could identify. The model is missing some of the characteristic features of a modern Faulcon deLacy production line. Instead, what once must have been a cargo hydraulics module was manufactured by BTS — Beerbaum and ThruSpace. It is a very peculiar model used only in the original production lines.”

“Never heard of them.”

“BTS was contracted by the original Anaconda manufacturer, RimLiner Galactic. RimLiner in turn was bought up by Faulcon Manspace in the early 2900s, before they even merged with deLacy Shipworks. This means our Anaconda is a very old model. Probably first or second generation. A barely visible ‘RLG’ marking on the bow corroborates this.”

Silence.  

“There is more,” Nadya added. “If it is a RimLiner model this would imply it must have had a Quirium hyperdrive with a probable maximum range of fourteen light years. Maybe twenty-eight before refueling. Sadly, the wreck’s drive section disintegrated. However, for all we know there was no means to manufacture Quirium outside of inhabited space. This means it is extremely unlikely this ship could have made it here on its own.”

“Oh, this get’s better and better,” Hermine sighed.

“A few weeks ago I would have said it’s absurd,” Jasmina conceded. “And yet, with all that we have met and seen along the way and the findings our brilliant minds came up with I’ll now put it in the realm of the possible.”

They looked at her.

“What? I mean I now believe in visions and hear ghosts talking and singing to me. This is just another day at the office for me now.”

“I guess we just found one of those expeditions that the Federal archives call the Missing,” Hermine said.

“In any case,” Jasmina continued, “I need to get a closer look myself. Captain Yuen, get the Quest ready and I think I will carry my little Quirium friend with me.”

She then turned to Delarue: “You have the flotilla. Take them along the expedition itinerary. I’m taking the Quest and scout ahead. I need to see a few things for myself. Or rather feel. I’ve been chained to the ready rooms for too long. We’ll meet up at the next waypoint. Until then, have the TeleTeam find out what they can.”

“Yessir!” Whether in earnest or in friendly mockery, DSS Operations Executive Hermine Delarue stood at attention and saluted, leaving an even more confused and head shaking Jasmina storm off the bridge.

“What do you mean, she’s gone?” Elaine Howard turned to Flight Lieutenant Moyers of Odysseus. “Of course she’s gone. She registered her FSD jump and flight plan a minute ago. What’s the problem?”

“Ma’am, I don’t know if it’s a problem at all.”

“Then why bother?”

“Sorry ma’am, maybe I should have been more precise. Miss Halsey’s ship left sensor range accordingly but… there is no high wake. ”

“What? Check your scanners, Moyers.”

“I just did, ma’am. Last known position at body 1a. Flight plan initiated at 03:47. Sensors still indicate a mass lock but I don’t have anything on scan.”

“Send out some Commanders. Tell them to sniff around, only in nicer terms.”

Eight minutes and thirty-two seconds later they had their reports: No ship, no mass lock and most importantly no high wake. But the very same location their “Lattice Signature” firmware picked up a massive… something. It was like a dissolving tissue in water, a fabric that had been there but now wasn’t. Something… fading? Or maybe closing?

“A portal collapse?” Elaine mused, looking at the data. “It’s similar to a carrier jump pattern. No conventional FSD spool-up and no high wake.” 

“But not even a Caspian can do that,” Moyers said.

“No. But maybe something else can.” 

Or someone… she thought. 

“Inform the fleet, ma’am?”

“Good heavens, no! This is opsec now, Moyers. Top team and Senior Carrier Command only. The last thing we need now is people getting agitated over Halsey disappearing again. There’ll be a bushfire of tinfoil, visions, spooky witch-space, Thargoids, Architects and whatnot. We had that already ten years ago. Just tell them Halsey is out in her Quest, scouting ahead.”

“Very well, ma’am. I’ll log the event as Alpha-1 confidential.”

---
In her Asp Explorer, the Inscrutable Sanctuary, Nadya entered a series of commands into the comms console. She was a jump out from the Ember, knowing the neutron star could have jeopardized the precision alignment of the Sanctuary’s FTL dish. After having entered a final code sequence the small holofac came to life, projecting the image on an old man. 

“Mir, earlier than agreed on. What is it?”

“We located the wreckage of an Anaconda out here in the Veils. There is credible proof it’s a RimLiner Galactic model from the 2900s. Corroded hull, missing drive section. There is a solid pattern now, John.”

“Interesting, so the Caretaker Protocol wasn’t shut down after all. I need to have a word with him about that. Did Halsey already send her weekly debrief to the Bubble?”

“No, she is scouting ahead in her Caspian. She is confused and I assume she wants closure or confirmation on her loose ends. She took the Q-Unit with her.”

“Good. Her debrief mustn’t go out. We have  enough trouble covering up Ram Tah’s interesting experiment. The chance of leaks is too great. See to it, Child.”

“Understood.”

And with that she terminated the short exchange and leaned back in her pilot seat, marvelling at the beauty of the Ember.
