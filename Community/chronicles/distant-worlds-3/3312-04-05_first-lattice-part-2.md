Meene, 3312 AD

ALERT! WORKSHOP SECURITY BREACHED! 
LOCKDOWN PROCEDURES IN PLACE! STANDBY FOR RESET… … …

“What the fuck!” Engineer Ram Tah screamed across the workshop comms system. “It tries to insert itself into the system. Tor, lock down prio one and two systems. Do it now!”

Looking through the plastisteel window of his workshop, Ram stared at the experiment he and Uwe had been setting up for two days: Embedded within what must have been hundreds of metres of cables and encased in a reinforced ceramic shell, a ribbed anthracite block, not larger than a conference suitcase, hung suspended in a magnetic field tube. 

There was no sign of activity whatsoever. And still that thing had sprung to a life of its own once it got its ‘incentive’.

Visibly alerted and perplexed, Uwe ran his fingers over the keyboard of not one, but three terminals. Red lights blinking here and there, interrupted by the occasional yellow and green. Subsequently a number of displays on the holoprojector wall went dark.

“Boss, I isolated life support and all external comms, broadband and FTL. Base integrity, landing pad force fields are intact. Up- and Download server isolated, base data seems secured.”

He looked up. “What is that?” 

Looking at the experiment again, the Engineer’s face showed raw bewilderment. He started to type a few sequences into a keyboard of his own, lining Trimex code row after row until finally hitting enter. Real programming. It was old school but he liked it that way. Kept things simple.

“I ran a system trace. The unit activated when we ran the frequency models. It then analyzed the workshop framework and got to work. But it’s not even interested in comms, operations or power. Wow! It’s… it’s trying to rewrite the matter synthesizer.”

“The what?! Shall I isolate?”

With his usual big ‘V’ brows Ram Tah hesitated for a moment, his face shifting from panic to fascination. “The matter synthesizer, Tor. Of all things. You remember our expedition friends mentioning they found decomposed matter? There is a purpose at work here, likely a very old one. What did radiocarbon say again?”

Uwe glanced briefly at one of the smaller workshop displays. “Extremely lower range so not older than 300 years.”  

“Boss!” he added nervously. “Shall I isolate? It’s compromising the workshop.”

“No. Shut down remaining priority one and two systems. Also, no docking permits and no customers. Tell them we’re under audit or something. And lay the synthesizer matrices on screen one.”

“Under audit? Are we?”

“Well, in a way I guess. That thing is starting to peek around.” He smiled his ‘I found something interesting’ smile, then he turned to his keyboard again. “Now, now. Let’s see what you’re up to.”

---
Telepresence. One of the marvels of the 34th century, but it had its limits. While it was possible to create a holo facsimile and talk face to face in this way, the extreme distance to the DW3 flotilla put a strain on the system each time they met. After all, all the signals still had to travel through hyperspace so there was a considerable delay and sometimes signal loss. So they had agreed to mostly use audio only.

‘They’ were Jasmina Halsey and Hermine Delarue from the DW3 “Odysseus” Flag Carrier, Astronomer Lya ter Holt from around Orion (hopefully) and Ram Tah from Meene in the Bubble.

The Engineer had called for an emergency meeting and he was just giving them the brief. 

“Remember a few days ago I said I needed to corroborate a few things,” he said. “I know I kept you in the dark but I had my reasons. Turns out they were not unfounded.”

“I need a bit more than that,” Jasmina interrupted. “There are people running around asking a lot of questions since we scrubbed that DSSA Carrier and found their evac ship. Even seasoned explorers get agitated when they feel they need to dump a half dissolved carrier crew and a vague cargo of something into a godforsaken crater in the middle of nowhere.”

“I know. But I have some answers. So hear me out first. We can then discuss later.”

“Very well.” 

“So it goes like this: Every once in a while an unremarkable courier transport with a very unusual cargo pops up near Shin Dez. No one quite knows where they come from and their allegiance callsigns are usually fake. But if you have friends with friends there, you’ll know.”

“Know what?”

“Know when they are underway. And as it happens, I have friends with friends.”

“Your army of ghosts?”

“Precisely.

“So what did they find out?” Hermine Delarue demanded. “I hope it all was worth it and that your hush hush didn’t link us to anything.”

“Rest easy. What we did is what we call an ‘isolated incident’.”

“Well, if they are transports, what do they transport then?” Lya asked

“Salvage mostly. Trinkets, personal effects of broken ships, the odd escape pod… But it’s a clever ruse. Most of these transports are decoys but some of them transport small, elusive technological marvels from ages past. They are indeed classified as AI Relics.”

“AI Relics?”

“Yes. Again no one knows. If you stop these ships they usually self-destruct and the crews, if alive at all, seem to have lost their memories.”

“This is getting wilder by the minute.”

“There is more,” the Engineer said. “These items are old, probably somewhere in the 250 years range, and if I recall you said that Dr. Reuben Hale from Tionisla said AI research wasn’t banned back then.”

“Yeah, GalCop. It’s always them in some way.”

“So while my ghosts chased down some of these relics I had the time and analysed the FSD mass telemetry you sent from your World of Death and the sector pattern from your Hunting of the Snark.”

“The Snark?”

“An incredibly ancient poem,” Lya interrupted cheerfully. “It was my idea! I love it. It’s about maps and beavers and searching for all kinds of absurd stuff.”

“Yeah, it all makes sense now…” a flabbergasted Hermine could be heard across the galaxy.

“It’s fascinating,” Ram continued. “You remember the Guardian ruins last November? Instead of using their million year old frequencies, they suddenly adopted a singular frequency in the picohertz range, source unknown. So we recorded the event.

Now, when you did your mass jumps at the World of Death there was a slight amplification in the exact same frequency range. And it’s interesting because the amplification wasn’t caused by your jumps per se but by that white dwarf. And that measurement was only possible because a good amount of your pilots mass jumped in direct vicinity of that dwarf. So you could say the dwarf helped catapult your ships on a teeny-weeny scale.”

There was an audible sigh. “Massive FSD engagements have often been speculated to create undesirable side effects in spacetime,” a weary Jasmina Halsey could be heard.

“That’s from the Federal Astronomical Journal!” Lya cheered. 

“Yeah, but picohertz is a bitch,” Ram spat. “After all, it's pretty close to gravitational waves. You need really dense matter. And had the distances been greater and hadn’t Lya been able to inject some of her ‘Temp’ equations with time being zero and whatnot, I probably wouldn’t have been able to detect them at all. But we now know where to look and I’m now able to run accelerated simulations so I can track them more easily.”

“So?” came Jasmina’s question.

“So? I had a hunch.”

“Oh, I guess this is going to be fun.”

“Well, sort of. After those AI Relics tried to usurp my lab…”

“They did WHAT?”

“Yeah, long story short, I hooked up one of those relics to my workshop where I usually fiddle around with Guardian based technology.”

“Are you fucking mental?” Hermine exploded.

“No, I’m Ram Tah. I need to keep my engineering weirdo reputation. So if you please let me finish: One of these relics tried to inject itself into my hardware. We managed to isolate and study it for a while. It didn’t try to usurp my base but it was very interested in my mass synthesizer. It began reprogramming the matrices.”

“But how is that possible?”

“I think because of an ancient inbuilt purpose. So I had another hunch.”

Sigh.

“I isolated the relic in a stasis unit and attuned it to the picohertz frequency we now can simulate and had it flown over to Jackson’s Lighthouse — a neutron star not so far away. A few friends then parked an old Adder right next to the relic.”

“Ooh! That’s so super smart of you, Ram,” Lya interrupted. “At the moment time is a constant in all equations and cannot fall below zero. If it could I would probably not date that Astrochemist on campus seven years ago. Come to think of it, time travel and if-clauses don’t really like each other. I have to write that down somewhere.”

“Lya, what’s your point?”

“Well… Umm… In a nutshell it means hypertemporal space is easier to cross into or out of when you are close to a hyperdense mass. Theoretically. It’s classic relativity with a few perks of my own. A bit like Albert ter Holt. Or Amanda Einstein. Or whatever. 

There is a lot of ‘hyper’ involved and those equations look more like ancient hieroglyphs,” she continued her cheerful rambling. “However, those new Caspians and their superduper-supercharges are the next best thing coming close. But they still can only extend jump ranges and not reduce hypertemporal transit time.”

“So what were the results at Jackson’s?” Jasmina asked.

“As I expected. When we deactivated the stasis pods the AI Relic started to get agitated. It tried to take over the Adder, drives and navigation first. At the same time we measured a spike in picohertz to the power of two hundred. It then started — and you’re gonna love this! — to reorganise the ship’s matter. Drives first, then hull, the computer, shipboard systems… Seems familiar?”

“Nanites?”

“No, not strictly. The polymers are deconstructed, reorganized and then consume other materials. There is purpose and function at work but after some time the newly formed matter just… decomposes. Decays. Loses its structure. We talked about that. I have never seen anything like it but maybe that’s why this AI thing was very interested in my mass synthesizer.”

“Ram, we have thousands of explorers out here. I need options and an action plan. We’re about to leave the Abyss and enter more frequently travelled space again. What’s your assumption?” 

“Jasmina,” the Engineer said gravely. “Simply speaking, whatever this is, it’s feeding on gravity waves, bleeds through, uses Guardian technology as a carrier and is able to reorganize matter on a ship-size scale.”

“Bleeds through what?” Hermine asked.

“Your witch-space,” Lya said happily. “Your friend lives in your witch-space. You’ve been talking about this since the beginning. Your voices, you always say they are not really here. Plus, it’s the only way Ram’s new friend can influence so many things on a galactic scale.”

After a pause she added: “If we ‘Temps’ as you call us are right, hyperspace exists in a zero time continuum. For maths it’s not a problem to handle three more dimensions. It’s just that FSD technology is transient and doesn’t let us stay there. But whoever or whatever actually does has all the options of an Altair Five-Card Draw.”

“This is what it meant back then,” Jasmina mused. “In those visions on Mars, and later in the Tionisla archives. Project Caretaker, the voices, the Missing. It’s Guardian and it was a plea for help.”

“Yes, we established that as a strong possibility. But not only that,” Ram added. “There must be another entity involved, and if it rides the Guardians as a carrier it probably has access to their remains and their resources. Take the obelisks for example. It supplanted their frequencies.”

“Something is broken in witch-space. That’s what it told me in my very first vision on Mars. Something wants to bleed out but it can’t. Not yet.” Jasmina mused. “We need to make sure we understand more. We need a new angle.”

“Yeah, good you have me,” Ram said. “I improvised a firmware patch, sending it to you now for fleet distribution. Tell your pilots to upload it to their sensor suite.”

“You think they will do it?”

“Why not? They have been downloading modified embedded firmwares, security firmware patches, cat media and settlement defense plans for years now.

Based on our models, it should help them detect frequency outbursts in their surroundings. Tell them to scan a few neutron stars to see if it works properly. Then I would suggest quietly doing a bit of sightseeing along your way. Look for wake irregularities when you mass jump, especially near neutron stars or white dwarfs. Neutrons preferably, since their distortion fields are much more dense.”

“You think that’ll work?”

“You’re on an expedition. Stop here and there, take a few selfies. Just look as if you belonged there, shouldn’t be that much trouble.”

“It’s been a while since I felt I belonged somewhere but I guess I can manage. ExComm out.”

Over telepresence Lya and Ram Tah exchanged some last concerns, shrugged unseen shoulders and terminated their connection.
