Faulcon DeLacy announced the Anaconda Mk II on 3312-10-20, the first major revision of the Anaconda ship model since its introduction in 2856. Chief of Design Frank Endis described it as "one of our finest large ships", emphasising that all potential roles — trading, mining, combat and exploration — received equal design attention. The ship ships with an SCO-optimised Frame Shift Drive, a Size 6 optional internal slot and Mk II armour slots and optimised hardpoints able to mount the new mining volley repeater, and was made immediately available as an early-release purchase.

Two days later, on 3312-10-22, the company launched a quadruple-focus field trial in partnership with several organisations to track ship performance data across mining, trading, combat and exploration, with pilots registering at HD 232819, AD Leonis, Reorte and HR 8061. Endis noted that while most new ship launches test only one or two capabilities, the Anaconda has always proven itself as an all-round vessel, making a comprehensive trial the logical next step toward wider shipyard availability.

On 3312-10-29, Faulcon DeLacy confirmed the trials had concluded successfully, with the data across all four initiatives showing independent pilots had put the new vessel through its paces across a number of activities. Endis described the Mk II as an extremely capable all-round ship with new armour upgrades and internal slot options, and the company confirmed the ship would transition from exclusive early-release purchase to limited shipyard inventories at a later date.
