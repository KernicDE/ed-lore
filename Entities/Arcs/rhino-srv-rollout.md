Wreaken Mining chief geologist Dr. Ben Curtis sparked a review of surface mining techniques in August 3312 with research proposing that modulated, tightly focused lasers could recover previously unobtainable resources from planetary surfaces. Wreaken began manufacturing deployable laser drills, refinery modules and modified Detailed Surface Scanners based on the work, and industry contacts anticipated a new surface-mining boom.

In September 3312, Vodel — the Core Dynamics-owned maker of the Scarab and Scorpion SRVs — unveiled the Rhino, the largest SRV available. Heavily armoured and sacrificing speed for superior grip, the Rhino requires a Large Vehicle Bay and carries deployable mining rigs as standard equipment through a joint venture between Vodel and Wreaken Mining, with an optional refinery module for on-site processing. Core Dynamics CEO Owen McKenna announced the venture.

Days later, Wreaken called for independent pilots to field-test the deployable mining rig, asking them to extract materials from various planetary body types and to laser-mine asteroids for comparative yield analysis. Pilots were directed to register at Metz Enterprise in the Ega system.
