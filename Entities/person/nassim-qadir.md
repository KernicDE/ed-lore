Nassim Qadir served as a scientist within the overlapping spheres of Azimuth Biotech and Aegis during the critical period of 3309, when both organisations pursued parallel tracks of anti-Thargoid research with radically different methodologies. His position placed him at the intersection of corporate biotechnology and tri-superpower military science, requiring him to navigate competing institutional priorities while contributing to humanity's defence against the alien invasion. The ten-month span of his documented activity coincided with some of the most consequential scientific developments of the Thargoid war.

Qadir's dual affiliations reflected the increasingly blurred boundaries between private sector research and official military programmes during the crisis. Azimuth Biotech's controversial history, including its predecessor organisation's role in the mycoid virus attacks, created persistent ethical tensions that scientists like Qadir had to manage in their daily work. His career illustrated how the urgent demand for anti-xeno solutions drew researchers into complex organisational environments where scientific objectivity competed with institutional loyalties and the pressure to produce results at any cost.
